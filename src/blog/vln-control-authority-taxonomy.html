Who Decides What Happens Next? A Control-Authority Taxonomy of VLN Systems

VLN systems describe themselves in architecture words — dual-system, slow–fast, planner, agentic. Those words answer the wrong question. For our recent paper on minimal-interface navigation agents (MIP [1]) we needed labels that survive contact with released code, and settled on two questions that do: at inference time, who decides what happens next — and where does the navigation competence live? The first sorts every system into policy, workflow, or agentic; the second into trained or zero-shot. Everything else a system carries — maps, memory, waypoint predictors, chain of thought — is equipment inside a cell, not a new cell.

1Two Questions, Six Cells

Control authority is the primary axis: at inference time, who decides what happens next. Three values.

  • Policy — one end-to-end model, invoked by an external loop once per control step. Observation and history in, action out. No orchestration across separately invoked components.
  • Workflow — a fixed, human-authored pipeline. Control passes among components in an order fixed by code; branch conditions are hand-written; the models fill slots in it.
  • Agentic — the model directs the loop itself: which action or tool to invoke, when to observe or re-plan, when to stop. The loop's shape is determined at run time.
note — "policy" here is a rough shorthand for the end-to-end model

Neighboring fields each read this word differently, so it is worth being explicit. In RL, a policy is any rule mapping observation (or state) to action — a property of the agent's behavior, so read that way an entire workflow would qualify: from the environment's seat, the whole pipeline is one obs → action function. Control theory's analogous object is the control law. And in the robot-learning literature the word has drifted onto the model itself — a "diffusion policy", a "visuomotor policy" — the trained network that emits the actions.

Our usage is that last one, and we state it roughly: by policy we mean one end-to-end model whose forward pass is the direct mapping from observation (and history) to action. The label sits on the model, not on the system boundary. This is a deliberately rough way of speaking — in the RL sense "policy" names behavior, not architecture — but it is the reading the taxonomy needs to keep the three classes disjoint: a fixed pipeline of frozen components also maps observation to action, yet no single model in it does, so it files under workflow. Internal structure does not break the label — caching, token merging, split computation inside one invoked model are still one policy; the seam that matters is a handoff between separately invoked components (§3).

Capability source is the secondary axis: where does the navigation competence live. Binary.

  • Trained — the weights were optimized on navigation data; competence is at least partly in the parameters.
  • Zero-shot — two checkable conditions, jointly:
    • the builder performs no weight update of any kind after the vendor's release — no navigation fine-tuning, nothing trained on the evaluation benchmark;
    • the checkpoint was released as a general-purpose model, not a navigation-specialized one — judged by the vendor's own positioning, the only public fact there is.
    Navigation behavior comes from eliciting what the released model already knows, through prompting and orchestration. Why the line sits at the released checkpoint — see the note below.
note — what "zero-shot" claims, and what it deliberately does not

The word used to carry three claims at once: I trained nothing for the task; nobody trained the model's navigation competence in; the model never saw this benchmark. In 2023 the three happened to coincide. They are coming apart: vendors will certainly tune navigation and spatial understanding in post-training — embodied applications are exactly where these models are heading — and no web-scale pretraining corpus can be certified free of a public benchmark's images. Only the first claim can ever be audited, so the label here keeps only that one, plus the general-purpose-release condition above.

The other two claims get handled differently, not folded into the definition. What the vendor's post-training installed is provenance: disclose it where known, mark it unknown where not — embodied data appearing incidentally in pre-training, generic spatial post-training, in-domain navigation SFT or RL are different rungs, and for closed models most rungs are unreadable. Crucially, when vendors do start installing embodied skill, a zero-shot system does not become invalid — the number it reports changes meaning, which is the point of a probe: it reads out how much embodied competence ships in general models at a given date. A zero-shot claim is therefore time-indexed; it names its checkpoint and its date.

Benchmark contamination, finally, is not a taxonomy question at all but an evaluation-validity one — it afflicts trained systems equally, since their base models also saw the web. Its remedies are empirical: new benchmarks, held-out splits, real-robot transfer. A definition that promises "never saw the test set" promises something no one can verify.

Two things fall out of the grid before any argument starts.

  • The placement rule — each label reflects the loop the system executes on its reported benchmark, not the diagram in its figure 1. A paper can ship a planner and never run it (§3 has a live example).
  • The empty corner — zero-shot × policy is empty for a structural reason: an end-to-end navigation policy just is competence in the weights, so a frozen generalist cannot be one. Its competence has to enter through scaffolding, which already makes it a workflow or an agent.

2The Test: Dynamic Control Flow, Not Dynamic Content

The workflow–agentic line is where classification usually goes wrong, so it gets one decisive test: dynamic control flow, not dynamic content.

  • Content — a different waypoint, subgoal, or frontier is a value in a fixed slot. The pipeline around it runs exactly as it would have run for any other value.
  • Control flow — agency requires changing what happens next: selecting a tool, taking a branch, deciding to re-plan. Sharpened: sequencing authority — agentic when the model's output determines which operation runs next and the harness is a pure executor of that choice. A flag the model produces but code consumes is still content.
  • Stopping shows the same line — a policy's STOP token is one more value handled by its external loop; an agentic stop terminates a loop the model could have continued.
  • Corollary: architecture depth is not control authority — a "brain", a planner, or a chain of thought adds computation inside the loop; it confers no agency while its role stays fixed.

3Why Dual-System Navigators Are Workflows

The trained dual-brain wave — ABot-N1 [10], InternVLA-N1 / DualVLN [11], Vesta [12] — is the case most easily misread as agentic, because the language-reasoning slow half looks like a planner. The division of labour is latency, not authority. The slow brain grounds the instruction and accumulated observations into a subgoal — a pixel goal, a latent plan, a chain of thought. The fast brain combines that subgoal with the live frame to produce short-horizon motion. They are decoupled to hit real-time control rates, not to let either restructure the loop.

Neither brain decides when the other runs. InternVLA-N1 hard-wires System-2 at roughly 2 Hz, System-1 at 30 Hz, its controller at 200 Hz; ABot-N1 caches the slow reasoner's pixel goal for its fast expert to track. The handoff — slow produces subgoal, fast executes subgoal — is the only route control ever takes, and it is fixed by code. That is a workflow, and joint training does not remove the seam.

One checkpoint, two cells. OmniNav [9] shows why the executed loop, not the checkpoint, carries the label. On R2R-CE it runs only its fast branch — one model mapping observations to waypoints — a policy. In its full exploration setting, a slow pass of the same VLM picks the next frontier and, once the target is detected, commits to it. The choice redirects the route, but it always fills the same prescribed slot: map construction, frontier generation, and the slow-to-fast handoff are fixed, and exploration ends because the fixed handoff executes the commit subgoal to arrival — not because the model exits a loop it owns. One architecture, two cells, agentic in neither.

4The Hard Calls

NavGPT [2] — the earliest zero-shot VLN system is also the first agentic one. It runs a ReAct [14] loop with a single movement tool: each turn the model returns an action or finish, and the loop continues only through that choice. Its stop ends a loop the model could have continued — agentic under the test. What makes people hesitate is its thinness: one tool, nothing else. By these axes, thinness is scaffolding width, not control authority, so NavGPT is recorded as an early, minimal instance of the cell rather than a canonical example. The rest of the zero-shot line that followed it — MapGPT [3], SmartWay [5], DiscussNav [4] — moved the other way, into fixed perception–map–prompt pipelines: zero-shot workflows.

planner × Qwen-RobotNav [8] — run bare on R2R-CE, Qwen-RobotNav is a trained policy regressing waypoints per step. But its call surface is parameterized — task mode, observation configuration — and in whole-system EQA deployments a frozen planner re-invokes it mid-episode and switches its modes. Under that orchestration the system is agentic, with the authority sitting in the planner and the trained policy serving as a tool. Same weights, two cells: labels attach to executed configurations, not to checkpoints.

AgenticNav [13] and our probe — the same cell, opposite ends of the scaffolding dimension. AgenticNav gives a frozen model depth queries, visual recall, pixel-grounded movement, and stop, built over a trajectory map, explicit visual memory, and geometric safety checks; the model calls them in any order, so it owns the sequence. The Minimal-Interface Probe [1] gives a general agent two functions — observe and act — and nothing else: no map, no memory, no waypoint machinery. On the same 100-episode R2R-CE board with the same nominal model, AgenticNav reports 55 SR / 48.41 SPL against the probe's 52 / 44.24 (serving paths uncontrolled, so no module-level conclusion). That gap is scaffolding, not control — which is why scaffolding stays a descriptive dimension rather than a third axis: it measures what a system carries, not who decides.

5How the Map Was Made

A taxonomy is cheap to assert and expensive to apply — every label above is a claim about somebody's code. The placements come from reading executed loops, at three depths:

  • Anatomy notes — for each of seventeen trained navigators, a per-system note built from the paper and its released code, recording the executed loop: what gets invoked, what passes between components on what schedule, who consumes the stop signal. That is where the 2/30/200 Hz numbers and the "caches the pixel goal" details come from.
  • A 37-method survey of the zero-shot line, with per-method code links.
  • End-to-end reproduction of several zero-shot systems on our own platform — the strictest reading of a paper there is.

Three house rules kept the labels honest:

  • Classify the trace, not the title — a paper named "agentic" can execute a workflow.
  • Unexecuted layers earn no credit — OmniNav's slow brain does not count on R2R-CE.
  • Multiple values allowed, vagueness not — one system may take two rows, each with its own evidence.

The full adjudications, including the borderline ones this post compresses, are in the taxonomy appendix of the paper [1].

6Position

Classify navigation systems by the loop they execute, not the architecture they draw. Two questions place every system we examined: who decides what happens next, and where does the competence live. Read the grid and the dual-system wave resolves into workflow engineering — real and useful, but not agency — while the zero-shot × agentic cell, open since NavGPT, is the one general agents now enter for free. The probe result says the entry fee is lower than the field assumed: a two-function interface with no navigation machinery lands within a few points of an engineered navigation harness on the same board. The burden of proof has moved to the scaffolding.
